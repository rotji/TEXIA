Yes. Now we move into **Phase 02 — Information Segmentation**.

This is where TEXIA starts doing something different from ordinary research: **we are going to break the narrative of each source into atomic information objects and preserve what kind of information each object actually is.**

# TEXIA TEST 001

## Phase 02 — Information Segmentation

### Our rule

For every source, we ask:

> **What exactly is this source giving us?**

Not:

> “What is the story?”

We classify each piece as one of:

```text
O = Observation
C = Claim
EV = Evidence
EVT = Event
Q = Qualification
I = Inference
H = Hypothesis
U = Unknown
```

And we preserve the source:

```text
S001 → O001
S001 → C001
S001 → Q001
...
```

---

# 1. S001 — State House Disclaimer

The June 11 State House notice says the government became aware that Adeniyi Adeyemi was presenting himself under PFIPC and portraying himself as appointed by the Chief of Staff. It says the purported office did not exist under the administration and that no appointment had been made. ([The State House][1])

Let's break that apart.

### O001

**Source:** S001
**Type:** Observation reported by source

> The Office of the Chief of Staff issued a public disclaimer concerning Adeniyi Adeyemi and PFIPC.

This is relatively straightforward.

---

### C001

**Claimant:** Office of the Chief of Staff

**Claim:**

> Adeyemi was portraying himself as having been appointed by the Chief of Staff.

**Claim type:** Institutional assertion.

**Status:** Reported claim.

Important:

We don't yet say whether the portrayal actually occurred independently.

---

### C002

**Claimant:** Office of the Chief of Staff

**Claim:**

> The purported PFIPC office did not exist under the administration.

**Claim type:** Institutional/legal-status assertion.

**Status:** Government assertion.

---

### C003

**Claimant:** Office of the Chief of Staff

**Claim:**

> No appointment had been made by the office.

**Claim type:** Institutional assertion.

---

### Q001

The notice specifically addresses:

* foreign/diplomatic missions;
* financial and multilateral institutions;
* development organizations;
* security agencies.

**Why this matters:** this tells us **which external institutional environments the government believed could potentially be affected.**

It does **not** prove that PFIPC successfully deceived all or any of them.

# 2. TEXIA immediately detects a missing piece

Look at this:

```text
C001
Adeyemi portrayed himself as appointed
          │
          ↓
C003
Chief of Staff says no appointment existed
```

We have competing propositions about an appointment.

But we don't yet have:

```text
APPOINTMENT DOCUMENT
       ↓
AUTHENTICITY TEST
       ↓
WHO CREATED IT?
       ↓
WHO RECEIVED IT?
       ↓
WHO RELIED ON IT?
```

Therefore:

### U001 — Open Evidence Gap

> What specific appointment document or representation was used by Adeyemi, and what evidence establishes its origin and authenticity?

That is a **TEXIA-generated research question**.

Excellent.

# 6. S003 — Budget Office statement

Now we reach one of the most interesting sources.

The Budget Office says that the National Assembly appropriated funds for **PEAC/PFIPC**, while emphasizing that appropriation is not itself a cheque, warrant, cash release or expenditure. It describes multiple controls that would have to be satisfied before money could move. ([The State House][3])

This source contains several distinct information types.

---

## C012

**Claimant:** Budget Office

> The National Assembly appropriated funds for PEAC/PFIPC.

This is a highly important claim.

Unlike the June disclaimer, this one concerns the **budget system**.

---

## O002

The Budget Office identifies an appropriation amount of:

**₦1,302,978,783**

and breaks it into:

* personnel: ₦802,978,783
* overhead: ₦200,000,000
* capital: ₦300,000,000. ([The State House][3])

These are reported figures.

We should later verify them directly against the **2026 Appropriation Act/budget documentation**.

So:

```text
O002
Source-reported budget figures

Verification status:
SECONDARY WITHIN CURRENT SOURCE
DIRECT PRIMARY DOCUMENT:
NOT YET VERIFIED
```

That distinction is important.

---

# 7. The crucial distinction

The Budget Office says:

```text
APPROPRIATION
      ↓
Financial Clearance
      ↓
Recruitment / Payroll
      ↓
Warrant
      ↓
Cash backing
      ↓
Payment
```

But different expenditure types have different pathways. ([The State House][3])

Therefore TEXIA creates:

### C013

> Appropriation does not itself mean money was released or spent.

### C014

> The PEAC/PFIPC personnel provision did not receive Financial Clearance.

### C015

> No lawful recruitment/payroll process followed.

### C016

> The overhead provision did not become a cash release.

### C017

> The capital provision did not become procurement expenditure.

These are all **Budget Office assertions about the financial process**.

They should not be merged into one statement.

---

# 8. We now have our first major state distinction

This is critical:

```text id="x6zv3b"
BUDGET STATE
     │
     │
     ↓
₦1.303bn APPROPRIATION
     │
     X
     ↓
NO EXPENDITURE
```

So:

> **“₦1.3 billion was budgeted”**

and

> **“₦1.3 billion was spent”**

are two completely different claims.

TEXIA must never collapse them.

# 9. But something even more interesting appears

The Budget Office says the council's institutional origins lay in the **Presidential Economic Advisory Council inaugurated in 2019** and that the Council had an administrative budget code. It also says establishment/recruitment instruments existed. ([The State House][3])

Now we have a new entity-resolution problem.

```text
PEAC
  │
  ?
  │
PFIPC
```

We cannot assume:

```text
PEAC = PFIPC
```

simply because the Budget Office writes:

> PEAC/PFIPC.

This must become an investigation question.

### U002

> What exactly is the relationship between PEAC and PFIPC?

Possibilities include:

```text
A. PEAC was renamed PFIPC
B. PFIPC was a mistaken label for PEAC
C. They were separate entities
D. A budget/document process conflated them
E. Another explanation
```

**Do not choose yet.**

This is precisely what entity resolution is for.

# 10. S004 — ICPC investigation

The ICPC's interim findings state that Adeyemi was not appointed by the Federal Government and that PFIPC was not created through law, executive order or another valid government instrument. The ICPC recommended prosecution and administrative sanctions while describing the investigation as continuing. ([The State House][2])

For TEXIA:

### C018

**Claimant:** ICPC

> Adeyemi was not appointed by the Federal Government.

### C019

**Claimant:** ICPC

> PFIPC was not established through law, executive order or another valid government instrument.

### C020

**Claimant:** ICPC

> Certain public officers' actions, omissions or negligence may have facilitated the operation.

Notice the wording.

**“May have facilitated”**

is not:

> “facilitated.”

This is an excellent example of why TEXIA must preserve **modal strength**.

---

# 11. TEXIA should preserve the strength of language

Consider:

```text
"caused"
"contributed to"
"may have contributed"
"possibly"
"allegedly"
"appears to"
"was found to"
"was established"
```

These are not interchangeable.

So we introduce:

## Claim Strength

Each claim should retain its original modality.

Example:

```text
C020

Original modality:
"MAY HAVE"

Normalized strength:
POSSIBLE / NOT ESTABLISHED

Do NOT normalize to:
"FACILITATED"
```

This is another useful TEXIA requirement emerging naturally from the investigation.

This qualification is extremely important.

# 13. What TEXIA must NOT do

A bad automated system might produce:

> “Adeyemi controlled 58 fraudulent bank accounts.”

That is **not what the source says**.

The source gives us:

```text
58 accounts
      +
identifying links
      +
preliminary investigation
      +
uncertain ownership/control
      +
no conclusion that all were unlawful
```

Therefore TEXIA must preserve:

```text
ACCOUNT LINK
    ≠
ACCOUNT OWNERSHIP
    ≠
ACCOUNT CONTROL
    ≠
ILLEGAL ACCOUNT
    ≠
FRAUDULENT TRANSACTION
```

This is an excellent real-world stress test.

# 14. First contradiction map

We now have something interesting.

### Question:

Did PFIPC have government legitimacy?

We currently have:

**C002 — State House:**
PFIPC did not exist under the administration. ([The State House][1])

**C005 — Presidency:**
PFIPC had no lawful government basis. ([The State House][2])

**C019 — ICPC:**
PFIPC was not created through law, executive order or another valid government instrument. ([The State House][2])

But:

**C012 — Budget Office:**
Funds were appropriated for PEAC/PFIPC. ([The State House][3])

These are **not necessarily contradictory**.

The current model is:

```text
             LEGAL EXISTENCE
                    │
                    ↓
             Apparently absent
                    │
                    │
                    ↓
             BUDGET APPEARANCE
                    │
                    ↓
                PRESENT
```

Therefore our central mystery becomes:

> **How did something that apparently lacked lawful establishment become represented within the federal budget architecture?**

That is a much stronger investigation than simply asking whether PFIPC was “fake.”

# 17. What has TEXIA discovered about itself?

This is actually the most important part of Test 001.

After analyzing only five source layers, we have already identified **five requirements that were not sufficiently explicit in our original V0.1 workflow**:

### Requirement 1 — Claim modality

Preserve words such as:

> allegedly, reportedly, may have, appears, confirmed, denied.

### Requirement 2 — Claim strength

Do not turn:

> “linked to”

into:

> “owned by.”

### Requirement 3 — Entity relationship uncertainty

Do not automatically equate:

> PEAC = PFIPC.

### Requirement 4 — Institutional pathway reconstruction

A legal entity question cannot be answered independently from:

* budget systems;
* documents;
* administrative processes;
* appointments;
* procurement;
* financial systems.

### Requirement 5 — Negative evidence

“No evidence that X happened” is not always equivalent to:

> “X definitely did not happen.”

TEXIA needs to distinguish:

```text
NO EVIDENCE FOUND
        ≠
EVIDENCE OF ABSENCE
```

This is a major discovery.
