# Story Structure

## Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway — Episode 1

### Working Title

**Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway: What Is Actually Being Built?**

---

# 1. Story Purpose

Episode 1 establishes the factual foundation for the investigation.

It should allow a viewer who knows little or nothing about the project to understand:

1. what the project is;
2. how it began in its current form;
3. what was approved;
4. what was contracted;
5. how financing developed;
6. how the project configuration changed;
7. what has physically been built;
8. what can be established as of 10 September 2026;
9. what remains unresolved.

The episode should **not attempt to solve every controversy surrounding the highway**.

Instead, it establishes the factual baseline from which later investigations can proceed.

---

# 2. Central Question

The entire episode is organized around one question:

> **What is actually happening with the Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway, and how has the project changed over time?**

This question is deliberately broader than:

> "Is the project good or bad?"

or:

> "Was the government right or wrong?"

Those are conclusions that should not be predetermined.

---

# 3. Narrative Principle

The episode follows:

```text
WHAT WAS PROPOSED
        ↓
WHAT WAS APPROVED
        ↓
WHAT WAS CONTRACTED
        ↓
WHAT CHANGED
        ↓
HOW WAS IT FINANCED
        ↓
WHAT WAS ACTUALLY BUILT
        ↓
WHAT EXISTS NOW
        ↓
WHAT REMAINS UNKNOWN
```

This gives the viewer a chronological and evidence-based reconstruction.

---

# 4. Opening — The Question

## 00:00–00:45

### Purpose

Create curiosity without making an unsupported accusation.

### Opening idea

The viewer sees:

* coastal highway footage;
* construction;
* road users;
* official project imagery;
* map fragments;
* different project descriptions;
* the "70%" figure;
* the 700 km / 750 km discrepancy.

The narration introduces the central problem:

> A major highway is being built along Nigeria's coast. Parts of it are already open to traffic, construction continues elsewhere, and the project has changed in important ways since it was announced.

Then establish the investigative question:

> So what exactly is being built?

### Important

Do not begin with:

> "The government changed the route."

Do not begin with:

> "Billions have disappeared."

Do not begin with:

> "The project is a success."

The evidence has not established those conclusions.

---

# 5. Chapter One — What Is This Project?

## 00:45–02:30

### Question

What exactly is the Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway?

### Establish

* federal infrastructure project;
* Lagos-to-Calabar coastal corridor;
* stated purpose;
* broad geographic scope;
* initial project descriptions.

### Visuals

* national/coastal map;
* project corridor;
* official project description;
* project photographs.

### Key message

The audience first needs to understand the object being investigated.

### Claim boundary

Do not yet attempt to explain every current section or exact route.

---

# 6. Chapter Two — How Did It Begin?

## 02:30–05:00

### Question

How did the current project formally begin?

### Chronology

```text
FEBRUARY 2024
FEC APPROVAL
       ↓
MAY 2024
FLAG-OFF
       ↓
SECTION 1 CONTRACT
```

### Establish

* FEC approval;
* initial Section 1;
* Hitech contractor;
* 47.474 km;
* contract scope;
* contract value;
* EPC + Finance structure.

### Visuals

* FEC evidence;
* contract/project documentation;
* Section 1 map;
* project timeline.

### Key distinction

Make clear:

> A contract is an agreement to carry out work. It is not evidence that all the contracted work has already been completed.

---

# 7. Chapter Three — The Project Did Not Stay Static

## 05:00–07:30

### Question

What changed after the project began?

This is where the audience first encounters the complexity.

### Establish

* project configuration changed over time;
* alignment alternatives existed;
* different project lengths have been reported;
* different section descriptions emerged;
* design and route decisions evolved.

### Visual

Show:

```text
EARLIER PROJECT DESCRIPTION
        ↓
DESIGN / CONFIGURATION CHANGES
        ↓
LATER PROJECT DESCRIPTION
```

Then introduce:

```text
≈700 KM
   vs
≈750 KM
```

### Important

Do not claim to know why the difference exists yet.

Instead say:

> The project is described differently in different documents. That difference is itself something the investigation needs to explain.

### Alignment treatment

Briefly show the ESIA's:

* First Proposed Alignment;
* Optimized Alignment.

Then state:

> These documents establish that alignment alternatives existed. They do not, by themselves, establish how every historical or legal route description corresponds to these lines.

### Result

The audience understands that "the highway" is not necessarily one fixed configuration that never changed.

---

# 8. Chapter Four — How Was It Going to Be Paid For?

## 07:30–10:00

### Question

How did the financing of the project develop?

### Establish

Initial:

> EPC + Finance

Then:

> financing difficulties

Then:

> government financing approach

Then:

> $747 million Section 1 financing close in July 2025

Then:

> approximately $1.126 billion associated with Phase 1 Section 2.

### Visual

Financial timeline.

### Critical explanatory moment

Introduce:

```text
CONTRACT VALUE
        ≠
FINANCING
        ≠
FINANCIAL CLOSE
        ≠
DISBURSEMENT
        ≠
EXPENDITURE
        ≠
VALUE OF WORK
```

### 30% statement

Present the June 2025 reported statement that approximately 30% of Section 1's contract sum had been disbursed.

But immediately qualify it:

> That is a reported statement. The underlying public cash-flow record needed to independently reconstruct exactly what was paid, when, and for what remains incomplete.

### Key message

The investigation is not alleging that the financing was misused.

It is demonstrating that **different financial numbers answer different questions**.

---

# 9. Chapter Five — Something Was Actually Built

## 10:00–13:00

### Question

What can we physically establish?

This is where the story moves from documents and announcements to the ground.

### Establish

* approximately 30 km commissioned in May 2025;
* approximately 30 km opened to traffic in December 2025;
* remaining Section 1 construction;
* Section 2 construction;
* construction activity beyond Lagos;
* other documented section progress.

### Visual sequence

```text
COMMISSIONING
      ↓
OPEN TO TRAFFIC
      ↓
CONTINUING CONSTRUCTION
      ↓
OTHER SECTIONS
```

### Show

* actual road;
* pavement;
* bridges;
* earthworks;
* traffic;
* construction footage;
* dated independent observations.

### Important

The physical evidence should make one thing obvious:

> This is not merely a project announcement. There is real physical construction.

But equally:

> Construction somewhere on the project does not mean the entire highway is complete.

---

# 10. Chapter Six — The Numbers Need Context

## 13:00–15:00

### Question

What do the project's reported progress numbers actually mean?

### Start with the September 2026 clarification

The reported 70% figure referred to Section 1, not the entire highway.

### Then demonstrate the problem

```text
"70%"
   ↓
WHAT SECTION?
   ↓
WHAT DATE?
   ↓
WHAT BASELINE?
   ↓
WHAT MEASUREMENT?
```

### Explain

A completion percentage without its:

* section;
* date;
* denominator;
* measurement method;

can easily be misunderstood.

### Visual

Large:

> 70%

Then underneath:

> **70% of what?**

### Key message

The investigation is not rejecting the number.

It is asking the more useful question:

> What exactly does the number measure?

---

### Purpose

---

# 12. Chapter Eight — Why the Investigation Continues

## 17:00–18:00

### Question

What remains worth investigating?

Introduce only the most consequential unresolved questions.

### Question 1

> Why did the stated project length change from approximately 700 km to approximately 750 km?

### Question 2

> How did the project's section and alignment configuration evolve?

### Question 3

> What can be independently established about actual financial disbursement and expenditure?

### Question 4

> What is the exact relationship between the different historical and engineering alignments?

### Question 5

> What is the actual current physical state of the entire corridor?

### Important

Do not turn these questions into accusations.

They are investigation targets.

---

# 13. Ending

The final message should return to the opening question:

> So, what is actually being built?

The answer at the end of Episode 1 should be:

> We can establish that a major federal coastal highway project was approved, contracted, financed through evolving arrangements, and physically constructed in multiple sections, with approximately 30 kilometres of the Lagos section opened to traffic.

Then immediately qualify:

> But the evidence also shows that the project's configuration has evolved, some headline numbers require context, and several important questions—including the complete current scope, the 700-to-750-kilometre discrepancy, detailed financial flows, and the exact historical alignment—remain unresolved.

Then:

> Those questions are where the investigation goes next.

---

# 14. What Episode 1 Does Not Attempt to Prove

Episode 1 should not attempt to determine:

### Alignment legality

Not yet.

### Whether particular demolitions were lawful

Not yet.

### Whether compensation was adequate

Not yet.

### Whether a particular private property was wrongly acquired

Not yet.

### Whether submarine cables actually intersected a particular route

Not yet.

### Whether money was misappropriated

Not established.

### Whether the project is ultimately successful or unsuccessful

Too early.

---

# 15. Narrative Tone

The tone should be:

### Curious

> "What changed?"

### Precise

> "This source establishes X, but not Y."

### Neutral

> "The government says..."

> "The available evidence shows..."

> "This remains disputed..."

### Transparent

> "We have not yet found the underlying document."

### Investigative

> "That creates another question."

Avoid:

* sensationalism;
* accusation without evidence;
* political cheerleading;
* political condemnation;
* artificial certainty;
* repetitive "breaking news" language.

---

# 16. Evidence-to-Story Relationship

The story should follow this hierarchy:

```text
PRIMARY EVIDENCE
      ↓
VERIFIED FACT
      ↓
NARRATION
      ↓
VISUAL
```

Not:

```text
INTERESTING STORY
      ↓
SEARCH FOR SUPPORT
```

The latter creates confirmation bias.

---

# 17. Contradiction Handling

When two sources appear to disagree, the episode should not immediately choose one.

Use:

```text
SOURCE A
  ↓
STATE A

SOURCE B
  ↓
STATE B

        ↓

ARE THEY ACTUALLY DIFFERENT?
        ↓
SCOPE?
DATE?
MEASUREMENT?
PROJECT STATE?
SOURCE LINEAGE?
        ↓
RESOLUTION
```

If the contradiction remains unresolved:

> Say that it remains unresolved.

---

# 18. Episode Pacing Principle

The episode should move between three modes:

```text
STORY
  ↓
EVIDENCE
  ↓
EXPLANATION
```

For example:

```text
THE ROAD IS OPEN
      ↓
SHOW ROAD
      ↓
SHOW OPENING EVIDENCE
      ↓
EXPLAIN:
30 KM ≠ 47.474 KM
```

Then:

```text
70% IS REPORTED
      ↓
SHOW STATEMENT
      ↓
EXPLAIN:
70% ≠ WHOLE HIGHWAY
```

This keeps the documentary understandable without sacrificing rigor.

---

# 19. Viewer Trust Mechanism

The viewer should repeatedly encounter:

> **Source**

> **Date**

> **Location**

> **What the evidence shows**

This should become a recognizable TEXIA Reports style.

The viewer should learn:

> When TEXIA says something is established, I can inspect why.

# 21. The Story's Central Arc

The episode should create one progression:

```text
A PROJECT
     ↓
BECOMES A CONTRACT
     ↓
BECOMES A FINANCING PROBLEM
     ↓
BECOMES A CHANGING DESIGN
     ↓
BECOMES PHYSICAL CONSTRUCTION
     ↓
BECOMES A PARTIALLY OPERATIONAL ROAD
     ↓
REVEALS MORE QUESTIONS
```

The final unanswered questions are not a failure of the episode.

They are the natural result of following the evidence.

---

# 22. Episode 1 Boundary

Evidence boundary:

> **10 September 2026**

Everything after this date belongs to future updates unless explicitly treated as later evidence.

Episode 1 therefore represents:

> **What the investigation can establish at the evidence boundary.**

It is not intended to be a permanent final account of the highway.

---

# 23. Final Story Principle

Episode 1 should leave the viewer with three things:

### 1. Understanding

They know what the project is and how it developed.

### 2. Evidence

They have seen enough primary/traceable evidence to understand why the major factual claims are being made.

### 3. Questions

They can clearly see what remains unresolved and why those questions matter.

The episode should therefore end not with:

> "Here is the truth."

but with:

> **"Here is what the evidence currently allows us to say—and here is where the evidence stops."**
