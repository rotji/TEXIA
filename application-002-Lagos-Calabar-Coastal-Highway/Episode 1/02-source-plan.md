# Episode 1 Source Plan

## Purpose

This document defines the sources used to investigate and verify Episode 1 of the Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway investigation.

It records:

* where information comes from;
* what type of source it is;
* what the source can establish;
* what the source cannot establish;
* whether information is independently verified;
* whether multiple reports originate from the same underlying source;
* important limitations and uncertainties.

The source plan is an investigation record, not a list of links for the audience.

**Evidence boundary:** 10 September 2026

---

# Source Priority

Sources are prioritized in the following order:

1. Primary official documents and records
2. Direct statements by institutions or identifiable actors
3. Project, contract, financing, procurement, environmental and legal records
4. Independent reporting with identifiable sourcing
5. Specialist analysis and technical context
6. Community, resident, eyewitness and stakeholder accounts
7. Social-media posts and other secondary material used primarily as leads

Source priority does not automatically determine truth.

An official source can establish what an institution announced or stated without independently proving that the underlying claim is true.

Likewise, a community statement can contain valuable information without automatically establishing the underlying event.

# Source Classes

## 1. Government Sources

Examples include:

* Federal Executive Council
* Federal Ministry of Works
* Federal Ministry of Environment
* National Assembly
* relevant state governments
* official government agencies

Primary use:

* project approvals
* official project descriptions
* contract information
* government decisions
* public announcements
* official construction updates
* environmental documentation
* institutional positions

Rule:

> Government sources establish what the government officially decided, announced, reported or documented. They do not automatically establish the truth of every underlying assertion.

---

## 2. Project and Contractor Sources

These include:

* contractor publications
* project documents
* engineering documents
* environmental studies
* project photographs
* construction updates

Primary use:

* design information
* construction scope
* technical descriptions
* project milestones
* engineering information
* physical construction evidence

Rule:

> A contractor source may provide direct evidence about its own work or project documentation, but claims about performance, completion or compliance may require independent verification.

---

## 3. Financing Sources

These include:

* financing institutions
* government financing announcements
* loan-closing announcements
* project-finance documentation where available

Primary use:

* financing commitments
* financing closure
* financing structure
* participating institutions
* announced amounts

Rule:

> Financing evidence must never be automatically interpreted as evidence of cash disbursement or expenditure.

Maintain the distinction:

```text
FINANCING ANNOUNCEMENT
        ↓
FINANCING CLOSURE
        ↓
DISBURSEMENT
        ↓
EXPENDITURE
```

---

## 4. Environmental and Technical Sources

The Section 1 ESIA is particularly important because it provides documented evidence about:

* project scope
* proposed alignment
* alignment alternatives
* environmental considerations
* social impacts
* infrastructure constraints
* stakeholder concerns
* engineering context

Important rule:

> An ESIA describes the project/design state documented by the assessment. It does not automatically establish the final constructed state.

---

## 5. Legal Sources

Legal records may include:

* court filings
* judgments
* court orders
* official legal publications
* litigation records

Primary use:

* existence of legal proceedings
* parties
* claims
* procedural history
* orders
* judicial findings

Critical distinction:

```text
PARTY'S ALLEGATION
       ≠
COURT'S FINDING
```

A filing can establish that a party made an allegation.

It does not establish that the allegation is true.

A judgment or order must be examined separately to determine what the court actually decided.

---

## 6. News Media

News reports are useful for:

* discovering events;
* locating statements;
* identifying documents;
* identifying affected parties;
* identifying dates;
* finding developments that may require primary-source verification.

News reports should not automatically be treated as independent confirmation.

For example:

```text
Government statement
       ↓
News outlet A
       ↓
News outlet B
       ↓
News outlet C
```

This is potentially one underlying source, not three independent confirmations.

---

## 7. Community and Stakeholder Sources

These include:

* residents;
* property owners;
* community organizations;
* local leaders;
* affected businesses;
* professional associations;
* project stakeholders;
* eyewitnesses.

These sources can be especially valuable for:

* identifying local events;
* locating disputed properties;
* identifying possible route changes;
* identifying people or documents requiring investigation;
* identifying events that may not yet be visible in official records.

However:

> Stakeholder testimony establishes that the person or group made the statement. It does not automatically establish the underlying event.

Different stakeholder groups may also have different interests and positions.

Therefore:

```text
STAKEHOLDER A'S POSITION
        ≠
STAKEHOLDER B'S POSITION
        ≠
"THE COMMUNITY'S POSITION"
```

---

## 8. Visual and Physical Evidence

This includes:

* photographs;
* videos;
* satellite imagery;
* maps;
* engineering drawings;
* construction footage;
* physical site observations.

Visual evidence can establish:

* what is visibly present;
* approximate physical condition;
* identifiable construction activity;
* spatial relationships where the source has adequate geographic reliability;
* changes between dated observations.

It does not automatically establish:

* ownership;
* legality;
* causation;
* financial value;
* responsibility;
* completion percentage;
* why a structure or road changed.

A visual record must therefore retain:

* source;
* creation/publication date;
* observation date where known;
* location;
* original context;
* limitations.

---

# Source Lineage

Every important claim should be traced toward its earliest identifiable source.

Example:

```text
ORIGINAL DOCUMENT
      ↓
INSTITUTIONAL STATEMENT
      ↓
NEWS REPORT
      ↓
SECONDARY REPORT
      ↓
SOCIAL MEDIA
```

The investigation should identify where possible:

* the original source;
* who first made the claim;
* when the information was first published;
* whether later reports independently verified it;
* whether later reports simply repeated it.

Repeated reporting does not equal independent corroboration.

---

# Source Verification Status

Use the following terms consistently.

### Located

The source has been found but not yet fully reviewed.

### Reviewed

The source has been examined but important verification work remains.

### Verified

The source has been reviewed and the relevant information is sufficiently supported for the stated proposition.

### Partially Verified

Some aspects are supported while other aspects remain uncertain.

### Disputed

Reliable sources or stakeholders materially disagree.

### Insufficient

The source exists but does not provide enough evidence to establish the proposition.

### Inaccessible

The source is known or referenced but the underlying material could not be accessed.

### Unresolved

Available evidence does not currently allow the investigation to determine the answer.

# Source Questions for Episode 1

The source collection should answer the following.

## Project Identity

* What is the officially defined project?
* What length was originally stated?
* What length is currently stated?
* Which sections exist at each stage?
* When did the section structure change?
* What documents define each configuration?

## Project History

* When was the project approved?
* When was it formally launched?
* What major decisions changed the project?
* When were the changes announced?
* What evidence establishes that the underlying state actually changed?

## Contract

* Who received the relevant contracts?
* What was the approved contract value?
* What was the scope?
* What procurement process was used?
* What contract structure was adopted?
* What financing obligations existed?

## Financing

* What financing was announced?
* What financing was formally closed?
* What was actually disbursed?
* What was actually spent?
* What payment records are available?
* Which figures are announcements rather than independently verified cash flows?

## Construction

* Which sections are physically under construction?
* What physical work can be independently observed?
* Which sections have documented completion?
* What does each reported completion percentage actually measure?
* Which sections were commissioned?
* Which sections were opened to traffic?

## Current State

* What can be independently established as of 10 September 2026?
* What is based only on government reporting?
* What remains disputed?
* What remains unknown?

# Important Source Gaps

The following evidence gaps remain important for Episode 1.

## 1. Complete financial flow

The investigation does not currently possess a complete public record showing:

```text
CONTRACT
   ↓
FINANCING
   ↓
DISBURSEMENT
   ↓
PAYMENT
   ↓
EXPENDITURE
   ↓
WORK VALUE
```

These should remain separate.

---

## 2. Exact project configuration history

The investigation has not yet obtained sufficient evidence to produce one definitive sequence showing exactly how the project changed from approximately 700 km to approximately 750 km and how all sections evolved.

---

## 3. Complete as-built construction data

Public visual evidence demonstrates construction activity, but a complete authoritative geospatial/as-built dataset for the entire project has not yet been obtained.

---

## 4. Exact historical alignment geometry

The investigation has identified references to historical/gazetted alignments and later alignment alternatives, but the exact geometry of every historical version has not yet been established.

This is particularly important for the later alignment investigation.

---

## 5. Independent measurement of construction progress

Government-reported completion percentages are useful evidence of what the government reports, but the underlying measurement methodology and independent verification are not always available.

---

## 6. Complete contract and procurement record

The investigation has established important contract information, including the Section 1 contract value and contractor context.

However, a complete publicly accessible procurement chain has not yet been assembled covering:

* procurement initiation;
* procurement approval;
* tender/procurement documentation;
* evaluation;
* award;
* contract execution;
* variations;
* amendments;
* payment certificates;
* subsequent contract changes.

Therefore:

> The existence and reported value of a contract should not be confused with a complete reconstruction of the procurement and payment history.

---

## 7. Complete financing-to-cash-flow record

The investigation has identified major financing developments, including:

* approximately $747 million Section 1 financing;
* approximately $1.126 billion Section 2 financing;
* later Phase II financing developments.

However, the complete chain from financing arrangements to actual disbursement and expenditure has not been publicly established.

---

## 8. Complete current construction inventory

The investigation has evidence of construction activity across several sections, but does not yet have a single authoritative dataset showing, as of 10 September 2026:

* exact completed kilometres;
* exact active construction kilometres;
* exact unfinished kilometres;
* construction status by chainage;
* independently verified completion percentage for every section.

# Source Handling Rules

## Rule 1 — Preserve original wording

Do not strengthen:

> "The minister said..."

into:

> "It was proven that..."

---

## Rule 2 — Preserve source modality

Maintain the difference between:

* said
* announced
* reported
* alleged
* claimed
* observed
* documented
* established

---

## Rule 3 — Publication is not proof

A report being published does not automatically establish the truth of the report.

---

## Rule 4 — Official is not automatically true

An official source establishes the official position or record.

It may still require independent verification.

---

## Rule 5 — Repetition is not corroboration

Ten articles repeating one government statement do not automatically equal ten independent sources.

---

## Rule 6 — Images have limits

A photograph or video may establish:

* something visible;
* a location;
* an approximate physical condition;
* a date, if independently established.

It does not automatically establish:

* why something happened;
* ownership;
* legality;
* financial value;
* completion percentage;
* causation.

---

## Rule 7 — Missing records remain missing

If the investigation cannot locate a document, record:

> Evidence not located.

Do not convert this into:

> The document does not exist.

---

## Rule 8 — Conflicting sources remain visible

Do not select one source simply because it produces a cleaner story.

Record:

* source A says X;
* source B says Y;
* evidence supporting each;
* whether either can be independently verified.

---

## Rule 9 — Source date and event date must remain separate

The date a source was published is not necessarily the date of the event it describes.

Every important source should therefore be evaluated for:

* publication date;
* event date;
* statement date;
* observation date;
* discovery date where relevant.

---

## Rule 10 — A source can establish a statement without establishing the underlying reality

For example:

> A government announcement can establish that the government announced a road alignment change.

It does not automatically establish:

> That the physical road alignment actually changed in the manner described.

The underlying physical state requires appropriate evidence.

---

## Rule 11 — No source should be treated as independent merely because it has a different publisher

Independence must be evaluated by lineage.

If several reports reproduce the same press release, interview or government statement, they are multiple publications of the same underlying information.

---

## Rule 12 — Evidence strength cannot exceed source capability

A source should not be used to support a proposition that it cannot technically establish.

For example:

```text
PHOTO
  ↓
VISIBLE CONDITION

NOT AUTOMATICALLY

PHOTO
  ↓
CAUSE
```

Similarly:

```text
NEWS REPORT
  ↓
REPORTED CLAIM

NOT AUTOMATICALLY

NEWS REPORT
  ↓
PROVEN FACT
```

---

# Source Lineage Integrity Check

Before using an important source in the documentary, ask:

1. Who originally produced the information?
2. When was it produced?
3. What exactly does it say?
4. What event or state does it refer to?
5. Is it primary or secondary?
6. Has the information been independently verified?
7. Are later reports simply repeating it?
8. What can the source establish?
9. What can it not establish?
10. Is there contradictory evidence?
11. Has the information subsequently changed?
12. Does the source describe an announcement, an observation, a claim, or an established event?

# Source-to-Episode Relationship

The source plan should support the public story without allowing the public story to determine the evidence.

The workflow is:

```text
SOURCE
   ↓
REVIEW
   ↓
CLAIM / OBSERVATION
   ↓
EVIDENCE ASSESSMENT
   ↓
TIMELINE / EVIDENCE PLAN
   ↓
PUBLIC STORY
```

Not:

```text
STORY IDEA
   ↓
SEARCH FOR SOURCES THAT SUPPORT IT
```

The investigation must remain evidence-led.

---

# Final Source Principle

The investigation does not ask:

> "How many sources say this?"

It asks:

> "What is the earliest identifiable evidence, what exactly does it establish, and how independently can we verify it?"

The objective is not to accumulate the largest number of sources.

The objective is to build a traceable chain from:

```text
SOURCE
  ↓
CLAIM / OBSERVATION
  ↓
EVIDENCE
  ↓
EVENT / STATE
  ↓
VERIFIED UNDERSTANDING
```

without silently removing uncertainty along the way.
