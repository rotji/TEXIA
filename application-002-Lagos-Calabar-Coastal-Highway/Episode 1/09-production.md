# Episode 1 — Production Plan

## Episode

**Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway: What Is Actually Being Built?**

## Purpose

This document explains how to turn the approved Episode 1 script into a finished TEXIA Reports video.

The goal is not to create a highly complicated production.

The goal is to produce a clear, credible and understandable video in which:

* the narration is understandable;
* the viewer can see the evidence being discussed;
* important claims can be checked;
* visuals help explain the story;
* uncertainty is clearly identified;
* the final video is good enough to publish.

---

# 1. THE BASIC PRODUCTION PROCESS

The production process is:

```text
APPROVED SCRIPT
      ↓
RECORD NARRATION
      ↓
COLLECT / PREPARE VISUALS
      ↓
BUILD VIDEO TIMELINE
      ↓
ADD NARRATION
      ↓
ADD VISUALS
      ↓
ADD MAPS / DOCUMENTS / GRAPHICS
      ↓
ADD SOURCE REFERENCES
      ↓
REVIEW
      ↓
EXPORT
      ↓
FINAL QUALITY CHECK
```

Do not try to do everything at once.

---

# 2. WHAT WE NEED TO PRODUCE

For Episode 1 we need:

### Audio

* Narration
* Optional low-volume background music
* Optional simple sound effects

### Visuals

* Maps
* Government documents
* Contract information
* Financing documents
* Construction photographs
* Construction videos
* Timeline graphics
* Charts/diagrams
* Screenshots of important evidence
* On-screen text

### Final elements

* TEXIA Reports identity
* Episode title
* Source references
* Closing screen

---

# 3. DO NOT START EDITING YET

Before opening the video editor and trying to build the entire episode, make sure we have:

* approved story structure;
* working script;
* evidence/source information;
* visual plan;
* basic production plan.

The production process should follow the evidence and script.

It should not determine the facts.

---

# 4. RECORDING THE NARRATION

The narration is the backbone of the video.

The viewer should be able to understand the story even if the screen temporarily contains only simple visuals.

## First recording

Do not worry about making it perfect.

Read the script naturally.

The first objective is:

> **Clear and understandable narration.**

Not:

> Perfect professional voice acting.

---

## Recording environment

Use the quietest practical room available.

Reduce:

* fan noise;
* television;
* conversations;
* traffic noise where possible;
* phone notifications;
* computer noise.

A quiet ordinary room is better than an expensive microphone in a noisy environment.

---

## Speaking style

The TEXIA Reports voice should be:

* calm;
* curious;
* clear;
* neutral;
* confident when evidence is strong;
* cautious when evidence is incomplete.

Avoid:

* shouting;
* exaggerated excitement;
* dramatic accusations;
* political campaigning;
* pretending to know something we don't know.

The tone should feel like:

> "Let's look at what the evidence tells us."

---

# 5. DO NOT RECORD THE ENTIRE EPISODE IN ONE TAKE

Record section by section.

For example:

```text
01-opening
02-what-is-project
03-how-did-it-begin
04-project-changes
05-financing
06-physical-construction
07-numbers
08-evidence-boundary
09-why-monitoring-continues
10-ending
```

This makes editing much easier.

If one paragraph is bad, we only replace that paragraph.

---

# 6. VISUAL COLLECTION

The visual plan tells us what visuals are needed.

Do not randomly download hundreds of photographs.

Every visual should have a reason.

For example:

### Narration

> "The first Lagos section covers approximately 47.474 kilometres."

Possible visual:

* Section 1 map
* 47.474 km displayed on screen

Not:

* random photograph of a highway.

---

# 7. TYPES OF VISUALS

We will mainly use six types.

## A. Maps

Used when explaining:

* location;
* route;
* sections;
* chainages;
* alignment;
* geographic relationships.

---

## B. Documents

Used when the actual document helps establish a claim.

Examples:

* FEC document;
* ESIA;
* contract document;
* financing announcement;
* government statement.

Show the relevant portion rather than displaying an entire document for too long.

---

## C. Photographs

Used to show physical conditions or events.

Examples:

* construction;
* flag-off;
* commissioning;
* road opening;
* bridges;
* construction sites.

---

## D. Video

Used when movement or physical observation matters.

Examples:

* vehicles using the completed road;
* construction equipment;
* pavement work;
* bridge construction.

---

## E. Graphics

Used when information is easier to understand visually.

Examples:

```text
CONTRACT
   ↓
FINANCING
   ↓
CONSTRUCTION
```

or:

```text
700 KM
   vs
750 KM
```

---

## F. On-screen text

Used to emphasize:

* dates;
* numbers;
* locations;
* important distinctions;
* unanswered questions.

Do not put the entire narration on screen.

---

# 8. DOCUMENT VISUALS

When showing a document, the viewer should be able to understand:

1. What document is this?
2. Who produced it?
3. When was it produced?
4. What part are we showing?
5. Why does it matter?

Example:

```text
FEDERAL MINISTRY OF ENVIRONMENT
ESIA — SECTION 1
0–47.5 KM
MAY 2024
```

Then highlight the relevant portion.

---

# 9. SOURCE REFERENCES

Important evidence should be identifiable.

A simple source label can appear at the bottom of the screen.

Example:

```text
Source: Federal Ministry of Works
Date: 26 February 2024
```

For a document:

```text
Source: Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway Section 1 ESIA
May 2024
```

The full source information will also be provided in the video description where appropriate.

---

# 10. SHOWING UNCERTAINTY

This is an important part of TEXIA Reports.

If something is uncertain, the visual should not make it look certain.

For example:

### Established

```text
ESTABLISHED
30 KM OPENED TO TRAFFIC
```

### Reported

```text
REPORTED
30% DISBURSED
```

### Unresolved

```text
UNRESOLVED
WHY DID THE PROJECT LENGTH
CHANGE FROM 700 KM TO 750 KM?
```

This helps the audience understand the difference between evidence and interpretation.

---

# 11. MAP RULES

Maps need special care.

A map must clearly indicate whether it is:

* official;
* historical;
* current;
* approximate;
* illustrative;
* reconstructed.

Do not draw a line on a map and present it as an official road alignment unless the evidence supports that.

For Episode 1, we should keep the alignment discussion relatively simple.

Detailed route reconstruction belongs to the later route-focused episode.

---

# 12. DOCUMENT SCREENSHOTS

When taking a screenshot from a document:

Keep enough surrounding information to establish context.

For example, don't crop a single sentence so tightly that the viewer cannot tell where it came from.

Prefer:

```text
DOCUMENT TITLE
        ↓
PAGE / FIGURE
        ↓
RELEVANT TEXT OR MAP
```

The original document should remain preserved in:

```text
assets/documents/
```

The edited screenshot can be treated as a presentation version.

---

# 13. VISUAL ASSET NAMING

Use simple names.

Example:

```text
VIS-001-project-corridor.png
VIS-002-fec-approval.png
VIS-003-section-1-map.png
VIS-004-contract-value.png
VIS-005-financing-747m.png
```

For video:

```text
VID-001-section-1-construction.mp4
VID-002-section-2-construction.mp4
```

For documents:

```text
DOC-001-section-1-esia.pdf
DOC-002-contract-document.pdf
```

The exact naming system can evolve later.

The important thing is that we can find things.

---

# 14. BUILDING THE VIDEO

The simplest editing structure is:

```text
VIDEO TRACK
    ↓
MAPS
DOCUMENTS
PHOTOS
VIDEO FOOTAGE
GRAPHICS

AUDIO TRACK
    ↓
NARRATION
    ↓
BACKGROUND MUSIC
```

The narration controls the story.

The visuals support the narration.

---

# 15. BASIC EDITING RULE

Ask this question constantly:

> **"Does this visual help the viewer understand what I am saying?"**

If yes:

Keep it.

If no:

Remove it or replace it.

Do not fill empty space with random footage simply because the screen needs something.

---

# 16. PACING

Do not leave the same photograph on screen for several minutes.

But also do not change images every second just to make the video exciting.

The visual should change when the information changes.

For example:

```text
Narration:
"On 26 February 2024..."

Visual:
FEC evidence

Narration:
"The first section covers 47.474 kilometres..."

Visual:
Section 1 map

Narration:
"The contract value was..."

Visual:
Contract/value evidence
```

The viewer should feel that the visuals are following the reasoning.

---

# 17. MAP ANIMATION

We do not need complicated animation.

A simple map can be enough.

For example:

```text
LAGOS
  ●
  │
  │ Section 1
  │
  │
  ↓
OGUN
  ●
```

Later, more sophisticated maps can be introduced if TEXIA Reports grows.

---

# 18. GRAPHICS

Graphics should be simple.

Example:

### Financial distinction

```text
CONTRACT VALUE
₦1.067 TRILLION

        ≠

FINANCING
$747 MILLION

        ≠

DISBURSEMENT

        ≠

EXPENDITURE
```

The purpose is understanding, not visual decoration.

---

# 19. MUSIC

Music is optional.

If used:

* keep it quiet;
* avoid dramatic thriller music;
* avoid music that tells the audience how they should feel about a person or event;
* lower or remove it during important evidence explanations.

TEXIA Reports should not sound like a crime documentary.

The evidence should create the interest.

---

# 20. THUMBNAIL ELEMENTS

Episode 1 thumbnail should be simple.

Possible elements:

* Lagos–Calabar highway image/map;
* road construction;
* short title.

Avoid putting the entire episode title on the thumbnail.

A possible direction:

> **WHAT ARE THEY BUILDING?**

with:

> **LAGOS–CALABAR HIGHWAY**

The final thumbnail will be developed separately.

---

# 21. FIRST EDIT

The first edit does not need to be beautiful.

Its purpose is to answer:

> **Does the story make sense?**

Check:

* Can I follow the story?
* Does the narration make sense?
* Do the visuals match the narration?
* Are there confusing jumps?
* Are important claims supported?
* Are there unnecessary sections?

---

# 22. SECOND EDIT

The second edit improves presentation.

Check:

* audio levels;
* visual timing;
* text size;
* map readability;
* document readability;
* transitions;
* pacing;
* unnecessary repetition.

---

# 23. FINAL FACT CHECK

Before export, stop editing creatively.

Switch into verification mode.

For every important statement ask:

```text
WHAT AM I SAYING?
       ↓
WHERE DID IT COME FROM?
       ↓
DOES THE SOURCE REALLY SUPPORT IT?
       ↓
AM I SAYING MORE THAN THE SOURCE SUPPORTS?
```

If the answer is uncertain:

* change the wording;
* qualify the statement;
* or remove it.

---

# 24. FINAL VIEWER TEST

Watch the complete video as if you know nothing about the project.

Ask:

### Can I understand what the project is?

### Can I understand what changed?

### Can I distinguish facts from reports?

### Can I see where important information came from?

### Can I tell what remains unknown?

### Does the video make me understand the project better?

If yes, the episode is doing its job.

---

# 25. EXPORT

The final video should be exported in a high-quality format suitable for YouTube and Facebook.

Keep:

```text
PROJECT FILE
+
MASTER VIDEO
+
THUMBNAIL
+
SOURCE MATERIAL
```

Do not delete the project files after publishing.

The project should remain editable because future information may require an update or correction.
