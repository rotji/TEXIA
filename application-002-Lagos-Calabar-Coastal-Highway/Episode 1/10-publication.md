# Episode 1 — Publication Plan

## Episode

**Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway: What Is Actually Being Built?**

## Evidence Boundary

**10 September 2026**

This episode represents the state of the available evidence at this point in time.

Later developments should not be silently added to Episode 1.

They should be documented as new developments and, when significant, become part of a later episode or update.

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF PUBLICATION

The purpose of publishing Episode 1 is to give the public a clear picture of:

* what the project is;
* how it developed;
* what has changed;
* what has physically been built;
* what the available evidence establishes;
* what remains uncertain.

The purpose is **not** to declare that the project is good or bad.

It is not to accuse anyone of wrongdoing.

It is not political campaigning.

It is not daily news reporting.

It is a public record of what TEXIA currently understands about the project.

---

# 2. WHERE TO PUBLISH

Initial publication platforms:

```text id="e4y3qf"
TEXIA REPORTS
      │
      ├── YouTube
      │
      └── Facebook
```

The same core episode can be published on both platforms.

The wording and formatting of the descriptions can be adjusted for each platform.

Do not try to build a website, newsletter, podcast, mobile application, or other platform yet.

Start with the two selected public channels.

---

# 3. EPISODE TITLE

Working title:

> **Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway: What Is Actually Being Built?**

The title should communicate the subject and the central question.

Avoid sensational titles such as:

* "The Lagos-Calabar Highway Scandal"
* "The Truth They Don't Want You to Know"
* "Government Exposed"
* "The Highway Disaster"

unless future evidence genuinely establishes something that warrants such language.

The title should create curiosity without telling the viewer what conclusion to reach.

---

# 4. THUMBNAIL

The thumbnail should communicate the subject immediately.

Possible main text:

> **WHAT ARE THEY BUILDING?**

Supporting text:

> **LAGOS–CALABAR HIGHWAY**

Possible imagery:

* actual construction;
* a relevant map;
* a documented section of the road.

Avoid:

* fake construction images;
* unrelated highway photographs presented as this project;
* exaggerated arrows;
* fake warning symbols;
* political imagery unless directly relevant.

The thumbnail should attract attention without misleading the viewer.

---

# 5. VIDEO DESCRIPTION

The description should explain what the episode does.

It should include:

1. short introduction;
2. what the episode examines;
3. evidence boundary;
4. important source links;
5. uncertainty statement;
6. TEXIA Reports identity.

The final publication description should be prepared after the source list is finalized.

Suggested structure:

This episode looks at how the project developed, what changed, how financing evolved, what has physically been built, and what the available evidence can — and cannot — establish.

This episode's evidence boundary is 10 September 2026.

We distinguish between established information, reported claims, observations and unresolved questions.

SOURCES

The final version should contain the actual links rather than placeholders.

---

# 6. SOURCE LINKS

The description should prioritize useful sources.

A good source list should normally include:

### Primary sources

* government documents;
* official project documents;
* ESIA;
* official financing announcements;
* court records where relevant.

### Important secondary sources

* reputable reporting;
* independent reporting;
* relevant technical publications.

Do not create a giant list of every article we read.

The objective is to allow viewers to inspect the important evidence themselves.

---

# 7. SOURCE ORDER

Where possible, organize the description like this:

```text
PRIMARY DOCUMENTS
    ↓
OFFICIAL STATEMENTS
    ↓
LEGAL / TECHNICAL RECORDS
    ↓
INDEPENDENT REPORTING
    ↓
OTHER RELEVANT SOURCES
```

This makes the source hierarchy visible.

---

# 8. DOCUMENT ACCESS

When a document is particularly important, make it easy for the viewer to identify.

For example:

```text
Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway
Section 1 ESIA
May 2024

Official source:
[LINK]
```

The viewer should not have to guess which document supports an important statement.

---

# 9. COMMENTS

Comments are useful, but they must be handled carefully.

A comment is not automatically evidence.

For example:

> "I live there and the road actually goes somewhere else."

This is potentially useful.

But it is still a lead.

We can ask:

> "Do you have a map, document, photograph, survey or other evidence showing that?"

---

# 10. COMMENTS AS LEADS

Use:

```text id="5bdk1z"
PUBLIC COMMENT
      ↓
POSSIBLE LEAD
      ↓
CHECK SOURCE
      ↓
COMPARE WITH EXISTING EVIDENCE
      ↓
IF VERIFIED
      ↓
UPDATE UNDERSTANDING
```

Do not automatically add comments to the factual record.

---

# 11. CORRECTIONS

TEXIA Reports should be willing to correct itself.

If we discover that a statement was wrong:

### Minor wording problem

Correct the relevant description or future publication.

### Important factual error

Clearly acknowledge the correction.

Example:

> **Correction:** An earlier version of this episode described the 70% figure as referring to the entire highway. The source actually referred to Section 1. The description has been corrected.

Do not quietly hide important corrections.

---

# 12. CORRECTION PRINCIPLE

The objective is not:

> "TEXIA must never be wrong."

That is unrealistic.

The objective is:

> **When TEXIA discovers that it was wrong, it corrects the record transparently.**

This is important for building long-term trust.

---

# 13. VIEWER CHALLENGES

Viewers may challenge the episode.

That is useful.

We should distinguish between:

### Challenge

> "Your claim is wrong."

and:

### Evidence

> "Your claim is wrong, and here is the official document showing why."

The second is much more useful.

---

# 14. HOW TO HANDLE DISAGREEMENT

When someone challenges a claim:

```text id="p1d6yt"
CLAIM
  ↓
CHALLENGE
  ↓
NEW EVIDENCE?
  ↓
YES → CHECK IT
  ↓
DOES IT CHANGE OUR UNDERSTANDING?
  ↓
YES → UPDATE
NO → KEEP CURRENT POSITION
```

We do not change a conclusion simply because somebody disagrees.

We also do not reject new information simply because it challenges what we previously believed.

---

# 15. PINNED COMMENT

A pinned comment can explain the purpose of the episode.

Suggested structure:

```text
This episode is a snapshot of what the available evidence allows us to understand as of 10 September 2026.

If you believe something in the episode is incorrect, please provide the source or evidence so it can be checked.

Useful evidence may include official documents, maps, court records, technical reports, dated photographs or independently verifiable observations.

Comments are useful leads, but claims are not automatically treated as established facts.
```

---

# 16. DO NOT ARGUE WITH VIEWERS

TEXIA Reports should not become a comment-section argument.

If somebody says:

> "You are lying."

We do not need to fight.

If somebody provides evidence:

> "Here is the government document."

We examine it.

The evidence is more important than winning an argument.

# 18. WHEN DO WE MAKE EPISODE 2?

We should not make Episode 2 simply because Episode 1 has been online for a certain number of days.

Episode 2 should happen when there is enough reason to go deeper.

For this project, the next planned subject is:

> **How did the highway's alignment change?**

Potential evidence could include:

* historical route documents;
* gazettes;
* survey plans;
* engineering drawings;
* ESIA alignment maps;
* court records;
* construction geometry;
* satellite imagery;
* cable/utility information.

If the evidence becomes strong enough for a meaningful episode, we publish it.

---

# 19. WHAT IF NOTHING IMPORTANT HAPPENS?

Nothing is wrong.

We do not need to manufacture content.

If there is no meaningful development:

> **No new episode.**

The existing episode remains part of the public record.

When something genuinely important happens, we return to the project.

# 24. PUBLIC POSITIONING

TEXIA Reports should describe itself simply.

### Short version

> **TEXIA Reports monitors real-world developments to understand what happened, what changed, what the evidence shows, and what remains unknown.**

### Longer version

> **TEXIA Reports is a public monitoring and inquiry project focused on understanding real-world developments through evidence, documentation and comparison over time. We document what can be established, distinguish reports and observations from stronger evidence, identify uncertainty, and update our understanding when meaningful new information appears.**
